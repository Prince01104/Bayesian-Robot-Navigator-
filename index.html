How many binsValidation accuracy

Accuracy rose quickly up to 10 bins and then flattened. This page uses 10 bins, which keeps the tables small enough to read. Test accuracy with 10 bins is shown above.

How it decides
  1. Each distance falls into one of 10 bins. The bin edges come from the training data only.
  2. For every action, multiply how common the action is (prior) by how likely each sensor's bin is when that action happens (likelihood).
  3. Divide by the total so the four numbers add up to 100%.
P(action | sensors) ∝ P(action) × Π P(binsensor | action)

What the model learned

Each row shows, for one action, how often the sensor lands in each bin. Bin 1 is the closest tenth of readings, bin 10 the farthest.

Data: UCI Wall-Following Robot Navigation, Freire, Veloso and Barreto (CC BY 4.0). The model is trained on the first 75% of the rows and scored on the last 25%. It assumes the four sensors are independent once the action is known, which is only approximately true.
